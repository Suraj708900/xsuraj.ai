What to improve before buying ads

Business blog · AI Suraj Teach Editorial · Updated October 2026

Before paying for Google or Facebook advertising, make sure visitors can understand your offer and contact your business. Sending paid traffic to a confusing page wastes opportunities even when the ad itself is relevant. Use the checks below to identify problems with your own website; results depend on your market and cannot be guaranteed.

1. Check your offer in ten seconds

Ask a person unfamiliar with your business to view the page for ten seconds. Then ask what you sell, who you serve, where you operate and what they should do next. If they cannot answer, improve the headline and supporting text. For example, “Book a website consultation for your restaurant in Patna” explains more than “Grow your business today.”

2. Test the contact journey yourself

On a phone, open the exact page your ad would link to. Fill the form with fictional test details and an email you control. Check that it accepts the input, displays a genuine confirmation and sends a notification to the team member who will reply. Test phone links only with a number your business owns. Set up a fallback contact method.

3. Match the ad to the landing page

If an ad promises a restaurant website quote, the landing page should explain restaurant website features and show the relevant enquiry option. Do not send people to an unrelated generic page. Avoid invented testimonials, urgency timers and claims that cannot be supported.

4. Inspect mobile usability and loading

Use a mid-range phone on a normal connection. Check whether text is readable, menu buttons open, fields are large enough to tap and images avoid unexpected layout shifts. Review performance with Google's PageSpeed Insights, then fix the actual issues it identifies rather than guessing at a score.

5. Know what a lead is worth

Define a qualified lead before spending: for instance, an enquiry from your service area with a real business need and a way to reply. Record the number of enquiries, qualified enquiries and paying customers separately. A cheap click is not necessarily a useful lead.

6. Add measurement with consent in mind

Decide what events you need to measure, such as a successfully submitted form. Follow applicable privacy and consent requirements before enabling analytics or advertising tags. Never record passwords, medical details or full form message contents as advertising events.

7. Start with a small controlled experiment

Choose one service, one audience and one clear landing page. Set a spending limit you can afford to lose. Check that your measurement works before interpreting results. Compare qualified enquiries, not just link clicks; avoid changing several variables at once.

Pre-advertising checklist

  • Visitors understand the offer and intended audience.
  • Pricing or the quotation process is clear.
  • Phone links and enquiry forms work on mobile.
  • A real person receives test submissions and can respond.
  • Ads and landing page describe the same service.
  • Privacy and basic business contact information are accurate.
  • Conversion measurement is tested, with appropriate consent.
  • The budget and stopping conditions are defined.

Example: a small salon

A salon wants more haircut bookings. First it creates a page showing the service area, opening hours, available services, how appointment requests are confirmed and a simple contact path. The owner completes a test booking request and checks that it reaches their inbox. Only then does the salon consider paid ads pointing to that page. This is a hypothetical example, not a customer success claim.

Frequently asked questions

Can ads fix a slow or confusing website?

No. Ads can send visitors to a website, but they do not fix usability or broken contact flows.

Should I buy ads before my form is tested?

It is better to verify that a visitor can submit an enquiry and your team receives it first.

How much should a new business spend?

There is no universal amount. Set a risk limit based on your finances and test process; spend nothing until the enquiry and measurement flows work.
